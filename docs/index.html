    Across Sweden

    The north is usually cheaper than the south

    Sweden is split into four price zones. Cheap hydropower sits in the north; the biggest demand is in the south.

    Spot price by zone

    Latest day · SEK/kWh

    Why is the north cheaper than the south? An electricity price comes down to three things: how much cheap power an area makes, how much it uses, and how well the grid can move power between areas.

    • The north makes more cheap power than it needs. Northern Sweden is full of hydropower (rivers and dams) that produces electricity very cheaply. The north uses only a fraction of it, and that surplus keeps the price low.
    • The south uses more than it makes. More people and industry live in southern Sweden, but it has far less cheap generation. So it needs more electricity than it can produce locally.
    • The power lines are the bottleneck. Cheap electricity has to travel south through the grid, but the lines between north and south have limited capacity. When they fill up, the south cannot get enough cheap power and turns to more expensive sources and imports, which pushes its price up.

    That is why SE1 and SE2 in the north are usually cheapest, and SE3 and SE4 in the south cost more. The gap shifts day to day with weather, demand and how full the lines are.
